# lc-lie-groups

By [@glaroton](https://github.com/glaroton).

**Lie groups modeled on locally convex spaces, formalized in Lean 4 and Mathlib.**

API documentation (generated by doc-gen4): <https://glaroton.github.io/lc-lie-groups/docs/>
Blueprint: <https://glaroton.github.io/lc-lie-groups/blueprint/>
(PDF: <https://glaroton.github.io/lc-lie-groups/blueprint.pdf>)

> **Status.** Early stage: Lie groups, their Lie algebras and the Lie functor, and `Diff(K)` for
> compact `K` as a Lie group. The library builds with no `sorry`, and its main theorems depend
> only on Lean's standard axioms. It is the third layer of a program on infinite-dimensional Lie
> theory: [lc-analysis](https://github.com/glaroton/lc-analysis) provides the Michal–Bastiani
> calculus, and [lc-manifolds](https://github.com/glaroton/lc-manifolds) the manifolds, the
> manifolds of mappings `C^ℓ(M, N)` and the composition map, and the fact that `Diff_{C^ℓ}(K)` is
> open in `C^ℓ(K, K)` for a compact manifold `K`.

## Convention: model spaces need not be complete

As in the companion libraries, model spaces are Hausdorff locally convex spaces, **not assumed
complete**, following Glöckner–Neeb [GN26, §5.7]. Results that need completeness (exponential
maps, regularity, integration of Lie algebra homomorphisms) state it as a hypothesis.

## Contents

### `LieGroup/Basic.lean`

* `MBLieGroup E G`: a manifold `G` modeled on the locally convex space `E`, with a group
  structure whose multiplication and inversion are smooth [GN26, Definition 5.1.1(b)].
* `MBLieGroup.smoothMBM_mul_left`, `smoothMBM_mul_right`, `smoothMBM_conj`: left and right
  multiplications and conjugations are smooth.
* `MBLieGroup.isTopologicalGroup`: a Lie group is a topological group.
* `MBLieGroup.IsHom`: morphisms of Lie groups, i.e. smooth homomorphisms
  [GN26, Definition 5.1.1(c)]; `IsHom.id`, `IsHom.comp`.

### `LieGroup/OfVaryingAtlas.lean`

* `IsVaryingLieGroup A`: the group operations of `G` are smooth for an atlas with varying models
  `A` (the kind of atlas that groups of mappings carry).
* `IsVaryingLieGroup.chartedSpace`, `IsVaryingLieGroup.hasGroupoid`, `IsVaryingLieGroup.mbLieGroup`:
  the translates `x ↦ φ(g⁻¹ x)` of the chart `φ` at the identity form an atlas modeled on a single
  space, and `G` is then a Lie group (the charts of the proof of [GN26, Theorem 5.3.1]).

### `LieGroup/OpenSubgroup.lean`

* `smoothMBM_subtype_val`, `smoothMBM_of_val_comp`: smooth maps and open subsets of manifolds.
* `OpenSubgroup.instMBLieGroup`: **open subgroups of Lie groups are Lie groups**;
  `OpenSubgroup.lieEquiv`: `L(U) ≅ L(G)`.
* `MBLieGroup.identityComponent`: the identity component `G₀` is an open subgroup, since Lie
  groups are locally connected (`locallyConnectedSpace_of_locallyConvex`).

### `LieGroup/Vector.lean` and `LieGroup/Prod.lean`

* `VectorGroup.instMBLieGroup`: **vector groups** `(E, +)` (as `Multiplicative E`, with the
  global chart `toAdd`) are Lie groups [GN26, Example 5.1.2]; `VectorGroup.lie_eq_zero`: their
  Lie algebras are abelian [GN26, Example 5.1.9(a)].
* `MBLieGroup.instProd`: **products of Lie groups are Lie groups**, modeled on `E × F`
  (lc-manifolds' product manifolds).
* `LocalGroupLaw.prod`, `LocalGroupLaw.bracket_prod`: products of local group laws have
  componentwise brackets; `MBLieGroup.LieAlg.prodEquiv`: **`L(G × H) ≅ L(G) × L(H)`** as Lie
  algebras.

### `LieGroup/Semidirect.lean`

* `SDProd α`, `SDProd.mbLieGroup`: **semidirect products** `N ⋊_α G` of Lie groups, for a smooth
  action `α` of `G` on `N` [GN26, Definition 5.2.7], on the product manifold `N × G` (a type
  synonym, with the group structure of Mathlib's `SemidirectProduct`). `SDProd.isHom_proj`,
  `SDProd.isHom_sec`, `SDProd.isHom_incl`: the canonical morphisms `π`, `σ`, `ι`, with
  `π ∘ σ = id` and `ι(N) = ker π` [GN26, Remark 5.2.10].
* `MBLieGroup.AdMulAut`, `MBLieGroup.isSmoothAction_AdMulAut`, `MBLieGroup.TangentGroup`: the
  adjoint action on the vector group `L(G)` is smooth, and `L(G) ⋊_Ad G` is a Lie group, the
  tangent group `T(G)` of [GN26, Example 5.2.12] (the identification with the tangent bundle is
  not formalized).

### `LieAlgebra/Semidirect.lean`

* `LocalGroupLaw.b2_semidirect`, `LocalGroupLaw.bracket_semidirect`: a local group law of the
  form `((x, y), (x', y')) ↦ (x ∗ α̃(y, x'), y ∗ y')` near the identity has
  `[(a, b), (a', b')] = ([a, a'] + D_b a' − D_{b'} a, [b, b'])`, with `D_b a' = ∂_y ∂_x α̃(1, 1)`.
* `SDProd.derivedAction`, `SDProd.bracket_eq`: **the Lie algebra of a semidirect product**,
  `L(N ⋊_α G) ≅ L(N) ⋊_D L(G)` [GN26, Proposition 5.2.8].
* `MBLieGroup.TangentGroup.derivedAction_eq`, `MBLieGroup.TangentGroup.bracket_eq`: for the
  adjoint action `D_b = ad b`, so the Lie algebra of the tangent group is `|𝔤| ⋊_ad 𝔤`
  [GN26, Example 5.2.12].
* `AffineGroup A E`, `AffineGroup.bracket_eq` (`LieAlgebra/Affine.lean`): for a continuous inverse
  algebra `A` acting continuously on `E`, **`E ⋊ A^×` is a Lie group with Lie algebra `E ⋊ A`**,
  `[(a, X), (a', X')] = (X a' − X' a, X X' − X' X)`; for `E` Banach and `A = L(E)` this is
  `Aff(E) ≅ E ⋊ GL(E)`, `aff(E) ≅ E ⋊ gl(E)` [GN26, Example 5.2.9].

### `LieGroup/Units.lean`

* `UnitGroup A`, `UnitGroup.instMBLieGroup`: **the unit group of a continuous inverse algebra is a
  Lie group** [GN26, Example 5.1.4], with the global chart `x ↦ x − 1` [GN26, Example 5.1.9(b)].
  Inversion is smooth by lc-analysis's `smoothMBOn_inverse` [GN26, Corollary 1.3.8].
  `UnitGroup A` is a type synonym for `Aˣ`, since Mathlib's charted space on the units of a
  complete normed ring uses the chart `u ↦ u`.
* `quasiLaw A`: the group law `x ∗ y = x + y + xy` on a (possibly non-unital) locally convex
  algebra [GN26, Definition 5.1.3(b)]; `quasiLaw_b2`, `quasiLaw_bracket`: `b₂(x, y) = xy` and
  `[x, y] = xy − yx`.
* `UnitGroup.lie_eq`: **`L(A^×)` is `A` with the commutator bracket** [GN26, Example 5.1.9(b)],
  using `LocalGroupLaw.bracket_congr` (the bracket only depends on the multiplication near the
  identity).

### `LieAlgebra/Local.lean`

* `LocalGroupLaw E`: a smooth local group law on `E`, such as the multiplication of a Lie group
  in a chart at the identity [GN26, Remark 5.1.8].
* `LocalGroupLaw.xl`, `LocalGroupLaw.xl_mul`: the local left invariant vector fields
  `x̃_l(z) = d(b ↦ z ∗ b)(1) x` [GN26, (5.4)], and their left invariance.
* `LocalGroupLaw.bracket`: the bracket `[x, y] = [x̃_l, ỹ_l](1)` [GN26, (5.5)];
  `localBracket_xl`: `[x̃_l, ỹ_l] = [x, y]~_l` near the identity [GN26, (5.3)];
  `bracket_jacobi`, `continuous_bracket` and bilinearity.
* `LocalGroupLaw.dMB_bracket_of_hom`: derivatives of local homomorphisms preserve brackets
  [GN26, Proposition 5.1.10]; `bracket_eq_zero_of_comm`: commutative laws have zero bracket
  [GN26, Example 5.1.9(a)].

### `LieAlgebra/Basic.lean`

* `MBLieGroup.LieAlg E G`: **the Lie algebra `L(G)`** [GN26, Definition 5.1.6], the model space
  `E ≅ T_1 G` with the bracket of left invariant vector fields computed in the chart at the
  identity; instances `LieRing` and `LieAlgebra ℝ`, and `LieAlg.continuous_lie`.
* `MBLieGroup.IsHom.lieHom`: `L(φ) = T_1(φ)` is a continuous Lie algebra homomorphism
  [GN26, Proposition 5.1.10]; `lieHom_id`, `lieHom_comp`: the Lie functor
  [GN26, Corollary 5.1.11].
* `MBLieGroup.LieAlg.lie_eq_zero_of_comm`: abelian Lie groups have abelian Lie algebras
  [GN26, Example 5.1.9(a)].

The proofs never use the tangent bundle of `G`: left translations relate the local fields
`x̃_l` to themselves, and lc-manifolds' transformation rule and Jacobi identity for local
brackets (`localBracket_transition`, `localBracket_jacobi`) do the rest.

### `LieAlgebra/Morphisms.lean`

* `MBLieGroup.IsHom.eq_of_lieHom_eq`: **morphisms of connected Lie groups are determined by their
  Lie algebra morphisms** [GN26, Proposition 5.5.7]; `eventuallyEq_of_lieHom_eq`: without
  connectedness, they agree near the identity. In the charts at the identities,
  `P = θ ∘ (φ₁ φ₂⁻¹) ∘ χ⁻¹` has `dP(χ(1)) = L(φ₁) − L(φ₂) = 0` (the multiplication has derivative
  `(a, b) ↦ a + b` and the inversion `−id` at the identity), and
  `P(z ∗ w) = (P₁(z) ∗ P(w)) ∗ ι(P₂(z))` gives `dP(z) ∘ d(z ∗ ·)(1) = 0` with `d(z ∗ ·)(1)` onto; so `P`
  is constant on a convex neighborhood, and the equalizer is an open, hence closed, subgroup.

### `LieAlgebra/Adjoint.lean`

* `MBLieGroup.Ad g`: **the adjoint representation** `Ad(g) = L(c_g)` for the conjugation
  `c_g(x) = g x g⁻¹` [GN26, Definition 5.2.4]; `Ad_one`, `Ad_mul`.
* `MBLieGroup.Ad_eq_id_iff`: **for connected `G`, `Ad(g) = id` iff `g` is central**
  [GN26, Corollary 5.5.8], from the uniqueness of morphisms with a given Lie algebra morphism.
* `MBLieGroup.dMB_Ad`: **`L(Ad) = ad`**, the derivative of `g ↦ Ad(g) y` at the identity in the
  direction `x` is `[x, y]` [GN26, Proposition 5.2.5]: in the chart, `Ad(g) y` is a derivative of
  the local conjugation `(a ∗ b) ∗ ι(a)`, whose mixed second derivative is `[x, y]` by polarization
  of the Taylor expansion (5.7).
* `MBLieGroup.comm_iff_lie_eq_zero`: **a connected Lie group is abelian iff its Lie algebra is
  abelian** [GN26, Proposition 5.5.9]: if `L(G)` is abelian, `L(Ad) = ad` and
  `Ad(g k) = Ad(g) Ad(k)` give `Ad = id` near `1`, hence on `G`, and `ker Ad` is the center.
* `MBLieGroup.smoothMBM_AdAction` (`LieAlgebra/AdjointAction.lean`): **the adjoint action
  `(g, y) ↦ Ad(g) y` is smooth** [GN26, after Definition 5.2.4]: near the identity,
  `Ad(φ⁻¹(z)) y = ∂_w K(z, φ(1)) y` for the local representative `K` of `(g, k) ↦ g k g⁻¹`, and at
  `g₀`, `Ad(g) y = Ad(g₀) (Ad(g₀⁻¹ g) y)`.
* `UnitGroup.Ad_apply`: for unit groups of continuous inverse algebras, `Ad(g) x = g x g⁻¹`
  [GN26, Example 5.2.6(a)].

### `LieAlgebra/LeftInvariant.lean`

* `MBLieGroup.leftField E x`: the left invariant vector field `x_l(g) = T_1(λ_g) x`
  [GN26, Definition 5.1.6] (`leftField_eq_tangentMap`, `leftField_one`), left invariant
  (`tangentMap_leftField`, [GN26, (5.2)]).
* `MBLieGroup.lieBracket_leftField`: **`[x_l, y_l] = [x, y]_l`** for lc-manifolds' bracket of
  vector fields [GN26, (5.3)], so the bracket of `L(G)` is the bracket of left invariant vector
  fields at the identity (`lieBracket_leftField_one`). In the chart at `g`, the left translation
  by `g` relates the fields `x̃_l` of the chart at the identity to `x_l`, which is the related
  vector field lemma [GN26, Lemma 3.4.17] in the form needed.

### `LieAlgebra/Taylor.lean`

* `LocalGroupLaw.b2`: the second order term `b₂(x, y) = dỹ_l(1) x` of the multiplication;
  `dMB_mul_one`, `dMB_dMB_mul_one`: `dm(1, 1)(x, y) = x + y` and
  `d²m(1, 1)((x, y), (x, y)) = 2 b₂(x, y)`; `b2_eq_mixed`, `continuous_b2`; and
  `bracket_eq_b2_sub_b2`: the bracket is the skew-symmetric part of `b₂`
  [GN26, Remark 5.1.8(a), Lemma 5.1.7].
* `LocalGroupLaw.LocalInverse`: a `C²` inversion `ι` near the identity. `dMB_inv_one`,
  `dMB_dMB_inv_one`: `dι(1) x = −x` and `d²ι(1)(x, x) = 2 b₂(x, x)` [GN26, (5.6)];
  `dMB_dMB_conj_one`, `dMB_dMB_commutator_one`: the conjugation `(x ∗ y) ∗ x⁻¹` and the
  commutator `x ∗ y ∗ x⁻¹ ∗ y⁻¹` have second order terms `y + [x, y]` and `[x, y]`
  [GN26, (5.7), (5.8)]. All from the second-order chain rule (lc-analysis, `dMB_dMB_comp`).
* `MBLieGroup.localInverse`, `MBLieGroup.LieAlg.dMB_dMB_commutator`: the inversion of `G` in the
  chart at the identity, and the commutator of `G` to second order.

### `Exponential/Basic.lean`

* `MBLieGroup.IsExp exp`: **exponential functions** [GN26, Definition 5.6.1]: `exp : 𝔤 → G` smooth,
  each `t ↦ exp(t x)` a one-parameter group (`IsExp.oneParam`), and `d(φ ∘ exp)(0) = id` in the
  chart `φ` at the identity [GN26, (5.14)]; `IsExp.exp_zero`, `IsExp.exp_neg`.
* Examples: vector groups, `exp = id` (`VectorGroup.isExp`, [GN26, Example 5.6.5]); unit groups of
  real Banach algebras, the exponential series (`UnitGroup.isExp_exp`, [GN26, Example 5.6.6]);
  products (`IsExp.prod`); an exponential function takes values in the identity component and is
  an exponential function of it (`IsExp.mem_connectedComponent`, `IsExp.toIdentityComponent`).
* `MBLieGroup.IsOneParam.eq` (`Exponential/Unique.lean`): **one-parameter groups are determined
  by their derivative at `0`** [GN26, Remark 5.6.4, Lemma 5.5.4]; hence `IsExp.unique`, **a Lie
  group has at most one exponential function** [GN26, Proposition 5.6.3], and `IsExp.map_hom`,
  **`φ ∘ exp_G = exp_H ∘ L(φ)`** [GN26, Proposition 5.6.12]. Instead of the logarithmic
  derivative, the proof works in the chart at the identity: for one-parameter groups `γ₁`, `γ₂`
  with derivative `x`, `φ(γ₁(−s) γ₂(s))` has derivative
  `−dm(x̃_l, 0) + dm(0, x̃_r) = 0`, both terms being the derivative of
  `t ↦ (a ∗ (1 + t x)) ∗ b = a ∗ ((1 + t x) ∗ b)`.

### `CircleCover/Periodic.lean` and `CircleCover/Group.lean`

* `CInfPer.D`, `CInfPer.smoothMBOn_mul`: on the Fréchet space `C^∞_per` of smooth `1`-periodic
  functions (lc-manifolds, `CInfPer`), the derivative is continuous linear and the
  multiplication is smooth.
* `CInfPer.smoothMBOn_precomp`, `CInfPer.hasDirDerivAt_precomp`: **the precomposition
  `(u, w) ↦ u ∘ (id + w)` is smooth**, with derivative `h ∘ (id + w) + (u' ∘ (id + w)) v`. The
  `O(t)` error of the difference quotients comes from Taylor's formula; higher derivatives
  follow by induction, and the derivatives of all orders through the closed embedding
  `C^∞_per → ∏ BC(ℝ)`.
* `CircleCover.coverLaw`: the group law `m(u, w) = w + u ∘ (id + w)` of the universal cover of
  `Diff⁺(S¹)` in the global chart `id + u ↦ u` [GN26, §13.3], a smooth global group law on
  `C^∞_per`; `CircleCover.coverLaw_bracket`: **its bracket is `[x, y] = x' y − x y'`**, the
  opposite of the bracket of the vector fields `x ∂_θ`, `y ∂_θ`.
* `CircleCover.smoothMBOn_η` (`CircleCover/Inverse.lean`): **the inversion
  `u ↦ (id + u)⁻¹ − id` is smooth on `Ω = {1 + u' > 0}`**. `G(u, y) = η(u)(y)` solves
  `σ + u(y + σ) = 0`, so it is smooth on `Ω × ℝ` by lc-analysis's implicit function theorem
  with locally convex parameters (`contDiffMBOn_of_implicit`); its `y`-derivatives restricted to
  `[0, 1]` are smooth by the superposition operators with parameters
  (`contDiffMBOn_superposeParam_of_contDiffMBOn`), and `u ↦ (u^{(m)}|_{[0,1]})_m` is a closed
  uniform embedding of `C^∞_per`.
* `CircleCover.CoverGroup.instMBLieGroup`, `CircleCover.CoverGroup.lie_eq`
  (`CircleCover/LieGroup.lean`): **the universal cover `\widetilde{Diff}⁺(S¹)` is a Lie group**
  modeled on `C^∞_per` [GN26, §13.3], **with Lie algebra `(C^∞_per, x' y − x y')`**. The group law
  printed in [GN26, p. 678], `m(f, g)(x) = f(g(x) + x) − x`, seems to be a misprint for
  `g(x) + f(x + g(x))`.
* `CircleCover.isExp_exp` (`CircleCover/Exponential.lean`): **`\widetilde{Diff}⁺(S¹)` has an
  exponential function**, `exp(x) = Fl^x_1`, the time-one flow of the periodic vector field
  `x ∂_y`. The flow is global by Picard–Lindelöf (Mathlib) with the bounds `|x| ≤ p₀(x)`,
  `Lip(x) ≤ p₁(x)` (`CircleCover.mem_dom`), and smooth in `(t, y, x)` by lc-analysis's maximal
  flows with locally convex parameters [GN26, Theorem 2.4.29] (`CircleCover.smoothMBOn_flow`);
  `CircleCover.smoothMBOn_of_uncurry` turns smoothness of `(x, y) ↦ Fl^x_1(y)` into smoothness
  into `C^∞_per`.
* `CircleCover.isC0Regular` (`CircleCover/Regular.lean`): **`\widetilde{Diff}⁺(S¹)` is
  `C⁰`-regular**. For `ξ ∈ C([0, 1], C^∞_per)` with `|ξ(t)'| < 1/4`, the solution of
  `u' = -ξ(t)(u)`, `u(0) = y` is the fixed point of a Picard operator on `C([0, 1], ℝ)`, a uniform
  family of contractions smooth in `((ξ, y), u)`, so it is smooth in `(ξ, y)` (lc-analysis,
  [GN26, Proposition 2.3.27(a)]); the evolution is `γ(t) = u_·(t)⁻¹`. The flow property
  `u_t = u_{s → t} ∘ u_s` writes `γ(t)⁻¹ γ(r)` as the time-one map of a rescaled field, whose
  derivative at `r = t` is computed by comparison with `exp`. The local criterion concludes.
  [GN26, §13.1] leaves the regularity of diffeomorphism groups to be written.
* `CircleCover.isHom_coverHom` (`CircleCover/Rotation.lean`, `CircleCover/Covering.lean`): **the
  covering morphism `\widetilde{Diff}⁺(S¹) → Diff(S¹)`**, `u ↦ (e^{2πit} ↦ e^{2πi(t + u(t))})`,
  is a morphism of Lie groups. The action on `S¹ ⊆ ℝ² ≅ ℂ` is `rotU(u, z) = e^{2πi u(arg z/2π)} z`,
  smooth on `C^∞_per × (ℝ² ∖ 0)`; the exponential law for `C^∞(S¹, S¹)` (lc-manifolds) and
  lc-manifolds' `smoothMBM_iff_contMDiffMB₂` make `u ↦ rotU(u, ·)` smooth into `Diff(S¹)`.
* `CircleCover.isC0Regular_diffCircle`, `CircleCover.isC0Regular_diffPlusCircle`,
  `CircleCover.isRegular_diffCircle`, `CircleCover.isRegular_diffPlusCircle`
  (`CircleCover/DiffCircleRegular.lean`): **`Diff(S¹)` and `Diff⁺(S¹)` are regular**. In the chart
  of `Diff(S¹)` at the identity, `⟪p, γ(p)⟫ > 0`, so `ρ(γ)(t) = arg(γ(e^{2πit}) e^{-2πit})/2π`
  is a smooth local right inverse of the covering morphism (`CircleCover.coverHom_rho`), and
  `MBLieGroup.IsC0Regular.of_hom_of_rightInverse` (`Regular/Hom.lean`) transfers `C⁰`-regularity
  from the universal cover.

### `Regular/Basic.lean`, `Regular/Evol.lean` and `Regular/Vector.lean`

* `MBLieGroup.HasLogDerivAt`: **logarithmic derivatives** `δ(γ)(t) = γ(t)⁻¹ γ̇(t)`, read in the
  chart `φ` at the identity: `s ↦ φ(γ(t)⁻¹ γ(s))` has derivative `δ(γ)(t)` at `t`.
* `MBLieGroup.eq_of_hasLogDerivAt`: **the Uniqueness Lemma** [GN26, Lemma 5.5.4]. Without the
  product rule: `γ₂ γ₁⁻¹` has vanishing logarithmic derivative because, near `t`, it is
  `K(w₂, w₁)` for a smooth `K` with `K(w, w) = 1`; and a curve with vanishing logarithmic
  derivative is locally constant.
* `MBLieGroup.IsC0Regular`: **`C⁰`-regular Lie groups** [GN26, Definition 6.1.1, `k = 0`]: every
  `ξ ∈ C([0, 1], 𝔤)` has an evolution (solved on `ℝ` for the extension of `ξ` by constants), and
  `evol : C([0, 1], 𝔤) → G` is smooth. Only `k = 0`, the strongest condition, is formalized:
  `C^k([0, 1], 𝔤)` for `k ≥ 1` is not yet a locally convex space in lc-analysis.
* `MBLieGroup.isC0Semiregular_iff`, `MBLieGroup.isC0Regular_iff` (`Regular/Interval.lean`):
  **these agree with the notions of [GN26, Definition 6.1.1]**, with solutions on `[0, 1]` and
  one-sided derivatives at the endpoints. A solution on `[0, 1]` extends to `ℝ` by the
  one-parameter groups `P(t) = γ(1)^⌊t⌋ γ(t − ⌊t⌋)` of `ξ(0)` and `ξ(1)`, glued by one-sided
  logarithmic derivatives (`HasLogDerivWithinAt.hasLogDerivAt_of_Iic_Ici`).
* `UnitGroup.isC0Regular` (`Regular/Units.lean`): **the unit group of a real Banach algebra `A` is
  `C⁰`-regular** [GN26, Theorem 6.3.4, for unit groups]. The equation `γ' = γ ξ` is linear:
  `γ = 1 + c` with `(1 − T_ξ) c = J ξ` in `X = C([0, 1], A)`, where `J η(t) = ∫₀ᵗ η` and
  `T_ξ c = J(c ξ)`, `‖T_ξ‖ ≤ ‖ξ‖`; so `c = (1 − T_ξ)⁻¹ J ξ` is smooth in `ξ` by the smoothness of
  the inversion of the Banach algebra `L(X)`, and the local regularity criterion applies.
* `MBLieGroup.isC0Regular_of_completeSpace` (`Regular/Banach.lean`): **Banach–Lie groups are
  `C⁰`-regular** [GN26, Theorem 6.3.4]. In the chart at the identity, with `c = z₀ + u`, the
  equation is `u = J(ψ(u, ξ))` for `ψ(a, v) = x̃_l(v)(z₀ + a)`; the inverse function theorem
  (Mathlib) applies to `Φ(ξ, u) = (ξ, u − J(ψ ∘ (u, ξ)))` on `C([0, 1], E)²`, whose derivative at
  `0` is `(h, k) ↦ (h, k − J h)` since `ψ(a, 0) = 0` and `ψ(0, v) = v`; the superposition operators
  are lc-analysis's. The logarithmic derivative of `γ = φ⁻¹(z₀ + u)` is computed with the left
  translation `b ↦ φ(g⁻¹ φ⁻¹(b))`, whose derivative inverts `x̃_l` at `φ(g)`. One-parameter groups
  come from small constant curves, and `MBLieGroup.isC0Regular_of_local'` (the local criterion
  with a `0`-neighborhood for each order `k`) concludes.
* `MBLieGroup.HasLogDerivAt.map`, `MBLieGroup.IsC0Regular.of_hom` (`Regular/Hom.lean`):
  `δ(φ ∘ γ) = L(φ) δ(γ)` for morphisms; **if `G` is `C⁰`-regular and `L(φ)` has a continuous
  linear right inverse `σ`, then `H` is `C⁰`-regular**, `evol_H(ξ) = φ(evol_G(σ ∘ ξ))` (one
  direction of [GN26, Proposition 6.1.5], for covering morphisms and more).
* `MBLieGroup.IsC0Regular.of_localDiffeo` (`Regular/Lift.lean`): **`C⁰`-regularity lifts along
  morphisms that are local diffeomorphisms at `1`** (the other direction of
  [GN26, Proposition 6.1.5]). For `ξ` near `0`, the evolution `Γ` of `L(φ) ∘ ξ` stays in the
  domain `V` of the local inverse `ρ` (`Γ(s) = evol(S(L(φ)ξ, s))` with `S(η, s)(t) = s η(st)`,
  small for balanced neighborhoods), and `ρ ∘ Γ` is the evolution of `ξ`, since
  `ρ(Γ t)⁻¹ ρ(Γ r) = ρ(Γ(t)⁻¹ Γ(r))` by injectivity of `φ` near `1` and `dρ(1) L(φ) = id`.
* `MBLieGroup.isC0Regular_openSubgroup_iff`, `MBLieGroup.isC0Regular_identityComponent_iff`
  (`Regular/OpenSubgroup.lean`): **an open subgroup, in particular the identity component `G₀`, is
  `C⁰`-regular if and only if `G` is**. The inclusion is the identity in the charts at `1`, so both
  directions of [GN26, Proposition 6.1.5] apply.
* `MBLieGroup.IsCkRegular`, `MBLieGroup.IsRegular` (`Regular/Ck.lean`): **`C^k`-regular and
  regular (Milnor) Lie groups** [GN26, Definition 6.1.1], on `C^k([0, 1], 𝔤)` and
  `C^∞([0, 1], 𝔤)` with the compact-open `C^k` and `C^∞` topologies (lc-analysis,
  `ContDiffLCMap`, `SmoothLCMap`). `MBLieGroup.IsCkRegular.mono`, `MBLieGroup.IsCkRegular.isRegular`,
  `MBLieGroup.IsC0Regular.isCkRegular`, `MBLieGroup.IsC0Regular.isRegular`: `C^k` implies `C^r`
  for `k ≤ r`, and `C⁰`-regular groups are `C^k`-regular and regular [GN26, Remark 6.1.2(c)].
  `Regular/Examples.lean`: vector groups of sequentially complete spaces, Banach–Lie groups, unit
  groups of Banach algebras and `\widetilde{Diff}⁺(S¹)` are regular.
* `VectorGroup.isCkRegular`, `VectorGroup.isRegular_of_mackeyComplete` (`Regular/Mackey.lean`):
  **`(E, +)` is `C^k`-regular (`k ≥ 1`) and regular for `E` Mackey complete** [GN26, Proposition
  6.1.3, one direction]: `C^k` curves are Lipschitz by the mean value inclusion on `[0, 1]`, and Mackey
  complete spaces integrate Lipschitz curves (lc-analysis).
  `VectorGroup.isCkRegular_iff`, `VectorGroup.isRegular_iff` (`Regular/MackeyConverse.lean`):
  **[GN26, Proposition 6.1.3]**, the converse included: if smooth curves on `[0, 1]` have weak
  integrals, `E` is Mackey complete (`MackeyConverse.mackeyComplete_of_integratesSmooth`, the
  equivalence of [GN26, Remark 1.1.18(c)] with [KM97, Theorem 2.14]), by lc-analysis's proof for
  Lipschitz curves with smooth bumps instead of tents, the coefficients decaying fast enough for
  the curve to be smooth where the bumps accumulate.
* `MBLieGroup.logDeriv`, `MBLieGroup.logDeriv_maurerCartan` (`Regular/MaurerCartan.lean`):
  **left logarithmic derivatives** `δF(q)(h)` of smooth maps `F : O → G` on open subsets of locally
  convex spaces, read in the chart at the identity; they are `C¹` and satisfy **the Maurer–Cartan
  equation** `d_k δF(h) − d_h δF(k) = [δF(h), δF(k)]` [GN26, Lemma 5.5.5]. In the chart,
  `dc(q) h = (δF(q) h)~_l(c q)` for `c = φ(F(p)⁻¹ F)`; differentiating at `p` and Schwarz's theorem
  give the bracket `dỹ_l(1) x − dx̃_l(1) y`.
* `MBLieGroup.exists_logDeriv_eq` (`Regular/Fundamental.lean`): **the Fundamental Theorem for Lie
  group-valued functions on convex sets** [GN26, Lemma 6.1.9, Theorem 6.1.13(i)], for `C⁰`-regular
  `G`: on an open convex `U ∋ 0`, a smooth `𝔤`-valued `1`-form satisfying the Maurer–Cartan
  equation is `δ(f)` for `f(y) = evol(t ↦ α_{t y}(y))`. The uniqueness lemma for `η' = [η, ξ]`
  [GN26, Lemma 6.1.7] (`MBLieGroup.eq_zero_of_hasDirDerivAt_bracket`) uses that `Ad(γ) η` is
  constant; instead of the two-parameter map of [GN26, Lemma 6.1.8] we compare `α` and `δ(f)` along
  rays (`MBLieGroup.hasDirDerivAt_ray`).
* `MBLieGroup.exists_logDeriv_eq_of_simplyConnected` (`Regular/FundamentalGlobal.lean`): **the
  Fundamental Theorem on simply connected open sets** [GN26, Theorem 6.1.13(i), (ii)(a)], for open
  subsets of locally convex spaces. As in [GN26, Proposition 6.1.10], the space of germs of
  primitives over convex open sets (`MBLieGroup.Germs`) covers `U`
  (`MBLieGroup.isCoveringMap_proj`), and the identity of `U` lifts (Mathlib's lifting theorem for
  simply connected, locally path connected spaces). The period map and `1`-forms on general
  manifolds are not formalized.
* `MBLieGroup.IsC0Regular.isExp`: **regular Lie groups have exponential functions**
  [GN26, Remark 6.1.2(a)].
* `VectorGroup.isC0Regular`: **`(E, +)` is `C⁰`-regular for `E` sequentially complete**, with
  `evol(ξ) = ∫₀¹ ξ` [GN26, Proposition 6.1.3].
* `MBLieGroup.isC0Regular_of_nhds` (`Regular/Local.lean`): **the local regularity criterion**
  [GN26, Lemma 6.1.4]: evolutions that exist and depend smoothly on `ξ` near `0` exist and depend
  smoothly on all `ξ`. Instead of cutting `ξ` into `N` pieces, we halve it repeatedly
  (`IsEvol.concat`, `evol(ξ) = evol(ξ₀) evol(ξ₁)`): if `ξ([0, 1]) ⊆ 2ᵐ W`, its halves take values in
  `2^{m-1} W`, and every `ξ` has compact, hence bounded, image.

### `Diffeomorphism/Basic.lean` and `Diffeomorphism/LieAlgebra.lean`

* `diffeos H K`, `diffeoAtlas A`: `Diff(K)` as an open subset of `C^∞(K, K)`, with the restricted
  manifold structure (`K` compact, `A` a local addition on `K`).
* `smooth_inv`: the inversion `Diff(K) → C^∞(K, K)`, `γ ↦ γ⁻¹`, is smooth
  [GN26, Proposition 13.1.10(a)]. By the exponential law this is [GN26, Lemma 13.1.8] for the
  evaluation map (lc-manifolds, `ContMDiffMB₂.inverse`), which rests on an implicit function
  theorem with locally convex parameters and finite-dimensional values (lc-analysis,
  `contDiffMBOn_of_implicit`).
* `isVaryingLieGroup_diffeos`: composition and inversion are smooth on `Diff(K)`.
* `Diffeo A`, `Diffeo.instMBLieGroup`: **`Diff(K)` is a Lie group**
  [GN26, Proposition 13.1.10(e)], modeled on `DiffeoModel A`, the smooth sections of `TK` along
  the identity (vector fields on `K`, in fiber coordinates).
* `Diffeo.toField_lie`: **`L(Diff(K))` is `V(K)` with the opposite bracket**
  [GN26, Example 5.4.3]: for a normalized local addition, `[x, y] = [Y, X]` for the vector fields
  `X`, `Y` with fiber coordinates `x`, `y` (lc-manifolds' `lieBracket`). Evaluation at a point
  commutes with the derivatives, so the bracket is a mixed second derivative of
  `(s, t) ↦ (s x ∗ t y)(p)`, computed in a chart of `K` (`Diffeo.evalAt_dMB_xl`); the
  normalization makes the second derivative of the local addition drop out.
* `UnitCircle`, `DiffCircle`, `DiffPlusCircle` (`Diffeomorphism/Circle.lean`): **`Diff(S¹)` and
  its identity component `Diff⁺(S¹)` are Lie groups** [GN26, §13.3], with `S¹ ⊆ ℝ²` and the
  normalized local addition of the sphere (lc-manifolds, `sphereLocalAddition`);
  `DiffPlusCircle.toField_lie`: **`L(Diff⁺(S¹)) ≅ V(S¹)^op`**.

## Planned

   built, as a Lie group morphism with a local right inverse; its kernel and the quotient are not).
   For `Diff(K)`: the finite-order statements of
   [GN26, Proposition 13.1.10(a), (b)] (`Diff_{C^{ℓ+k}}(K) → Diff_{C^ℓ}(K)` is `C^k`), and the
   compatibility of the translated atlas with the restricted atlas of `C^∞(K, K)`.
2. Lie algebras: `L(Diff(K))` as a Lie algebra isomorphism with `V(K)^op`, once `V(K)` is a Lie
   algebra type in lc-manifolds.

   inverse algebras [GN26, Example 5.6.9]. Regularity: regularity of `Diff(K)`, and the Fundamental
   Theorem for `1`-forms on manifolds and the period map [GN26, Theorem 6.1.13(ii)(b)].

   Lie algebra (the general Finsler theory is in lc-manifolds).
